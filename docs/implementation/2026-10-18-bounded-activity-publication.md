# Bounded Activity publication and recovery completion — 2026-10-18

Status: production implementation and final verification complete. Development version **0.1.0**, SQLite schema **10**. No Git repository or applicable AGENTS.md exists in this workspace or its ancestors. Settled contracts are unchanged.

## Audit and implementation

The remaining Activity writers could delete an arbitrarily large staging set, ingest arbitrary per-PR histories and publish all summary membership/catch-up progress in a single transaction. Activity publication also ran retention inside that transaction, adding up to three mutations per expired entry. Notification target publication trusted the provider's requested page size without validating the returned cardinality. The shared detail/API writer used by Activity hydration could add its three writes to a separate 128-mutation admission allowance.

Covered transactions now commit **at most 128 direct mutations total**, including publication bookkeeping, revision changes and the shared admission contribution:

- First-page Activity initialization deletes abandoned per-PR staging in batches of at most 128. New staging commits at most 100 records per transaction, reserving the remaining allowance for admission. A failed later batch can retain unpublished staging; it cannot publish Activity, successful progress or eligibility. Startup discards staging if no durable publication owns it. Duplicate individual source IDs still reject the traversal.
- Completed Activity sets of at most 16 staged records retain one atomic transaction. The conservative worst case is 100 direct publisher mutations (six per record plus one grouped intent, target, progress and revision), leaving the remaining allowance to shared admission. Larger sets use the existing prepared publication journal.
- Recovery completion with at most 54 catch-up keys retains one transaction, guarded by the same 128 maximum. Its worst case is 112 mutations: a summary, 54 members, 54 catch-up deletions, baseline, cycle and revision. Larger sets use the journal, including empty-membership/historical-baseline completion.
- Notification discovery validates at most the requested 50 source threads. At most 50 deduplicated targets, successful discovery progress, notifications-complete and revision commit together (at most 53 direct mutations). Oversized pages cannot advance those states. Existing server deadlines and exact-URL validators remain separate and authoritative.
- The shared detail/API writer now reserves its three publication mutations from admission's 128 allowance. This covers Activity source/owner payloads as well as ordinary detail cache writes. Independent reclamation retains its existing global 128-mutation bound.

The 16/54/100 thresholds choose transaction implementations; they impose no cap on retained Activity, watches, traversal history or summary members. Recovery initialization and watch enrollment retain their already-bounded implementations.

Expiration no longer piggybacks on Activity publication. Existing read-time 90/30-day filters continue to apply without writes, and periodic/startup/repair retention reclaims expired minimal entries and payloads. Durable seen/dedup/intent/attempt barriers survive. Broader retention/control-plane transaction bounds remain open; this slice does not claim to bound them.

## Atomic visibility and logical completion

`activity_publication.rs` adds Activity and recovery-summary plans to the shared publication engine. All classification reads the original ledgers during preparation, before any dedup application. The journal fixes occurrence cutoff, baseline, recovery mode/boundary, Pause/manual policy, publication time and the next intent identity. Only fresh eligible unseen records enter catch-up or one ordinary per-PR intent. Expired records establish durable dedup/seen barriers without resurrecting minimal records or payloads; historical and suppressed records retain their existing policies.

Every plan/application batch uses the existing twelve-row identity limit and combined admission guard. Payload plans refer to Activity staging rather than copying bodies into new plan values. Applying a payload moves it from staging in the same transaction as its applied bit. All retained old payload images remain charged against the **524,288,000-byte inclusive shared ceiling**. UTF-8/BLOB accounting, protected staging and independent LRU ordering remain unchanged. Metadata/journal overhead is measured managed-file space, not a whole-database-size promise.

The actual-ceiling reader audit found SQLite materializing the general protected content view, including unrelated payloads, when joining an Activity page. A correlated wrapper still allowed an automatic covering index to retain that materialization. Activity now applies the same canonical/old-image visibility predicates at the exact payload key inside each union branch; EXPLAIN confirms indexed `main.content(key)` and `publication_row(tbl,k)` lookups. Clear/PR/check-denial and access-age guards remain outside those branches. Functional page/fault/restart assertions are unchanged. A real stack sample of the unchanged 257-record/full-ceiling fixture also located repeated overflow-page reads in admission expiry. Schema 10 adds the access-time index, and expiry uses a range predicate rather than a nullable OR, so empty expiry passes can use that index. Pressure selection, candidate limits, ordering and mutation accounting are unchanged; the full 257-record and 524,288,000-byte fixtures are retained. This targeted plan correction does not establish a general query/temp/memory/time or native performance bound.

The protected snapshot now covers Activity, seen, dedup, hydration targets, baseline/catch-up progress, cycle, eligibility intents and membership, alongside the existing content/watch/check tables. Before the single visibility/revision commit, application readers see the complete previous state. They cannot observe a new baseline, completed recovery, partial summary membership or a terminal intent missing members. After that commit, they see the complete new state even if bounded journal reclamation is interrupted. Raw `main.*` inspection is physical state, not an application reader surface.

Exact Activity request identity survives staging consumption. Retrying an interrupted request finishes its persisted publication and returns without a second empty publication or revision. Repeated source hydration preserves the durable source-ID barrier and cannot recreate terminal eligibility. A repeated recovery-completion call finishes its own pending summary journal. Other pending publication kinds remain readiness blockers and are resolved only by their normal retry/startup/repair paths. Seen updates also resolve publication before writing through protected views.

The existing runtime serialization, storage-error stop and cached-reader protection apply to the new kinds. Startup and repair resume the journal before abandonment cleanup, retention, recovery initialization, identity validation or remote eligibility. Failed repair stays stopped; successful repair preserves persistent Pause, original stable identity and polling deadlines. No native delivery backlog is introduced: new eligibility remains terminal `unavailable`, and existing `attempted`/`unavailable` records and attempt/membership history remain terminal.

## Schema compatibility

Schema **10** is a journal-protocol compatibility barrier and adds the `content_lru(accessed,key)` index; it adds no new tables. Schema-9 binaries lack the Activity protected-reader definitions and must reject schema 10. Schemas 0–9 upgrade in the existing fully synchronized single atomic migration, with admission before every step and commit. Historical schema reconstruction fixtures explicitly remove the new index before emulating schemas 1/3/9. The expanded deterministic migration matrix comprises **170 actual SIGKILL cases and 130 injected space failures**, in DELETE/WAL modes. Results are recorded below. Native migration and signed compatible/incompatible binary replacement remain Pending.
